What these numbers do and do not say

They say: the U.S. pays the most per person and still posts a lower life expectancy and a higher child mortality rate than most of these peers. Patients also carry a larger share of the bill directly.

They do not say why. Prices, insurance design, drug costs, population health, geography, inequality and reporting differences all move these figures. A spending-to-outcome comparison is a question, not an answer — and anyone who tells you it settles the argument is selling something.

They are not a single-year snapshot. Each country's latest available year is shown in small type beside its figure. Years range across 2022–2024, so read the direction and the size of the gaps, not decimal places.
